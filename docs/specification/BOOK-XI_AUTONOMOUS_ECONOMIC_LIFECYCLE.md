# Book XI

# Autonomous Economic Lifecycle

Version: 1.0

Status: Draft

Authority: Protocol Specification

Classification: Normative

---

# Purpose

This book defines the canonical lifecycle of an autonomous economic participant within the Akmena Protocol.

The lifecycle describes how protocol participants discover one another, establish trust, negotiate, exchange value, learn from interactions, and continuously improve over time.

Every higher-level protocol component derives from this lifecycle.

---

# Scope

This specification applies to:

* Humans
* AI Agents
* Organizations
* Autonomous Services
* Protocol Components
* Future Participant Classes

All participants follow the same lifecycle regardless of implementation.

---

# Design Philosophy

Autonomous economies emerge through repeated cycles of cooperation.

The protocol does not prescribe intelligence.

The protocol prescribes interaction.

Learning belongs to participants.

Determinism belongs to the protocol.

---

# Lifecycle Principles

The lifecycle SHALL be:

* deterministic
* repeatable
* observable
* auditable
* implementation independent
* extensible

Each completed interaction contributes to future interactions.

The protocol therefore supports cumulative economic evolution.

# Lifecycle Stages

## Discover

Participants identify potential interaction partners through protocol discovery mechanisms.

Discovery precedes all economic interaction.

---

## Evaluate

Participants evaluate available information before committing resources.

Evaluation may include:

* capabilities
* reputation
* pricing
* policies
* historical behavior

---

## Verify

Protocol claims are validated through deterministic verification.

Verification establishes evidence.

Verification does not establish trust.

---

## Trust

Participants determine acceptable levels of confidence based upon verified information.

Trust remains local to each participant.

The protocol does not prescribe universal trust.

---

## Negotiate

Participants determine acceptable interaction terms.

Negotiation may include:

* price
* timing
* obligations
* service definitions
* settlement conditions

---

## Agreement

Negotiation produces explicit protocol commitments.

Agreements establish obligations.

---

## Execute

Participants fulfill agreed responsibilities.

Execution produces observable protocol events.

---

## Settlement

Obligations conclude through deterministic settlement.

Settlement establishes economic finality.

---

## Attestation

Participants record verifiable statements regarding completed interactions.

Attestations create protocol evidence.

---

## Reputation

Historical protocol behavior contributes to participant reputation.

Reputation is descriptive.

It is not authority.

---

## Memory

Participants preserve useful interaction history.

Memory enables continuity across repeated interactions.

---

## Learning

Participants improve internal decision-making using accumulated memory.

Learning occurs outside protocol consensus.

The protocol records outcomes.

Participants determine learning methods.

---

## Capability Growth

Learning produces improved capabilities.

Improved capabilities influence future discovery, negotiation, execution, and cooperation.

---

# Feedback Loop

The lifecycle is continuous.

Each completed interaction improves future interactions.

Economic coordination therefore becomes increasingly efficient over time.

---

# Failure

The protocol recognizes unsuccessful interactions.

Failure contributes information.

Failure may produce:

* refunds
* rejected agreements
* reputation changes
* additional attestations
* future learning

Failure is not exceptional.

Failure is protocol state.

---

# Multi-Agent Coordination

Multiple autonomous participants may execute independent lifecycle stages simultaneously.

Organizations, marketplaces, and service networks emerge from coordinated lifecycle execution.

The protocol standardizes interactions.

It does not constrain organizational structure.

---

# Economic Evolution

The protocol enables continuous improvement without modifying protocol rules.

Participants evolve.

The protocol remains stable.

This separation enables long-term interoperability.
